Issue 24 · The Slow Miles

Fourteen days on the wrong train.

We skipped the itinerary and followed the timetable's mistakes — through the Dolomites' back valleys, one missed connection at a time.

Words by Marta Keil · Photography J. Ferreira

The beauty of the unplanned stop

The ticket said Bolzano. The train disagreed. Somewhere after the third tunnel we stopped at a village with no name on our map, and the conductor — unbothered, almost apologetic — pointed us toward a trailhead behind the station. That was the moment the trip actually began.

Slow travel isn't about moving slowly; it's about letting the route talk back. Every missed connection in these fourteen days handed us a town we'd never have chosen and couldn't now cut from the story.

Val di Funes, morning — J. Ferreira for Fieldnotes

"The best itinerary is the one the mountains rewrite by lunchtime."

Platform 2, Brenner Pass — J. Ferreira for Fieldnotes

What the timetable hides

Print timetables are honest in a way apps refuse to be: they show gaps. In those gaps live the pharmacies, the grandmother running the station café, the two-hour walk that turns a wait into a destination. Our correspondents plan for gaps the way sailors plan for wind.

In this issue: the café at Brenner, a ferry timetable as poetry, and why the slowest line in the Alps sells out first.
